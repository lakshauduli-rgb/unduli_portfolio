Semi-Monocoque Sailplane Wing Design

An ongoing individual engineering project developing a sailplane wing from aerodynamic configuration through structural design, material selection, and validation.

Project Overview

This is a standalone ongoing individual project focused on the design of a sailplane with a two-spar semi-monocoque wing. The objective is to maximise aircraft range while balancing aerodynamic performance, structural strength, stiffness, mass, material selection, and manufacturability.

The work is divided into two connected phases: Phase 1 establishes the aerodynamic geometry and operating baseline, while Phase 2 develops the structural wing box and evaluates its materials and sizing requirements.

Design Constraints

  • Wingspan range: 12-18 m; selected preliminary span: 15 m.
  • Aspect ratio: 24; taper ratio: 0.8.
  • Root chord: 0.694 m; tip chord: 0.556 m.
  • Fuselage length restricted to 45-55% of the wingspan.
  • Total aircraft mass target: 250-300 kg; selected design mass: 275 kg.
  • Maximum allowable wing mass: 110 kg.
  • Maximum wing-tip deflection: 5% of span = 750 mm.
  • Calculations are carried out using MATLAB and engineering analysis methods.

Phase 1 - Aerodynamic Design

Status: Completed

Phase 1 established the aerodynamic configuration with the goal of maximising aircraft range. Candidate airfoils including FX67K170, FX73K170, FX60126, FX61184, and GOE533 were compared using a weighted decision matrix.

  • Lift-to-drag ratio weighting: 50%.
  • Manufacturability weighting: 30%.
  • Lift coefficient weighting: 20%.
  • FX73K170 selected with a weighted score of 4.4.
  • Matched condition: α = 3.5°, CL = 0.975, CD = 0.00768, CL/CD = 126.95.
  • Reported operating result: α = 2.5°, CL = 0.8710, CD = 0.00731, CL/CD = 119.15.
  • Generated lift reported as 2701.27 N.

Phase 2 - Structural Design and Material Selection

Status: In progress

Phase 2 is a separate structural development stage for the individual wing project. It translates the Phase 1 aerodynamic geometry into a two-spar semi-monocoque wing box and establishes the methods required for structural sizing and validation.

  • Structural idealisation of spars, spar caps, webs, skins, ribs, and stringers.
  • Spanwise aerodynamic and structural load distributions are being developed.
  • Bending is assigned primarily to booms and spar caps; shear is assigned to skins and spar webs.
  • Euler-Bernoulli beam theory is used for spar bending.
  • Thin-walled theory is used for skin and web shear sizing.
  • Flat-plate buckling is used for skin panels and Euler column buckling for stringers.
  • Local flange buckling and crippling checks are included for Z-section stringers.
  • Front spar search range: 10-30% chord; second spar placement range: 40-80% chord.
  • Minimum wing-box separation: 20% chord; target cross-product inertia Ixy = 0.

Materials and Current Progress

  • 7075-T6 aluminium for spar caps, stringers, and ribs: density 2810 kg/m³ and tensile yield strength 635 MPa.
  • 6061-T6 aluminium for wing skins and shear webs: density 2700 kg/m³ and tensile yield strength 276 MPa.
  • Upper spar caps are treated primarily in compression and lower spar caps in tension.
  • Completed so far: structural idealisation, load assumptions, preliminary sizing criteria, buckling methods, mass targets, and material selection.
  • Remaining work: final structural sizing, refinement, trade-off comparison, and validation.
