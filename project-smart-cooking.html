Smart Cooking System

A semi-automated kitchen system that measures ingredients, dispenses recipe quantities, controls cooking, and communicates with a mobile application.

Jan 2025 - May 2025 Mechatronics / IoT Group project
Project file PRJ-03 Primary platform Mobile app / ESP32 / Arduino Project type Group project My contribution Embedded hardware, load-cell calibration, dosing logic, and Bluetooth integration My role Embedded systems and calibration developer Status Completed

Overview

The Smart Self Cooking Kitchen System was developed as a group project for ME 2041. Its purpose was to automate key stages of cooking: ingredient measurement, ingredient dispensing, heating, stirring, temperature maintenance, and automatic shutdown.

The system combines a mobile user interface with a distributed embedded control system. A user selects a food category, recipe, and quantity through the app, while the hardware coordinates the measuring, dispensing, and cooking sequence.

My Contribution

I contributed to the embedded hardware and calibration side of the project. My work focused on load-cell-based ingredient measurement, the dosing logic used to calculate and release quantities, and the integration of the ESP32 and Arduino control hardware.

  • Calibrated the load-cell measurement system for ingredient weighing.
  • Developed mass-measurement and proportional dosing logic.
  • Worked with Arduino and ESP32 hardware integration.
  • Supported communication between the mobile interface and the controller system.
  • Contributed to testing the actuator and ingredient-container mechanisms.

System Architecture

  • Mobile application: Provides food category, recipe, quantity selection, and cooking controls.
  • ESP32: Receives commands from the mobile application over Wi-Fi and passes control data into the embedded system.
  • Master Arduino: Coordinates the overall cooking sequence and communicates with the slave controllers.
  • Spice slave Arduino: Controls the stepper motor and screw mechanism used for spice dispensing.
  • Liquid slave Arduino: Controls pumps and monitors the liquid dispensing system.
  • Actuators and sensors: Linear actuators, stepper motors, pumps, ultrasonic sensors, a float switch, load cell, hot plate, stirrer, and K-type thermocouple support the physical process.

Development Process

  1. Define the cooking objective. Broke the cooking task into measurable and controllable stages: weighing, dispensing, heating, stirring, temperature maintenance, and shutdown.
  2. Design the user workflow. Structured the mobile interaction around pairing, recipe selection, quantity selection, dispensing, and cooking commands.
  3. Build the measurement stage. Placed the empty pot on the measuring area and used the load-cell system to establish the required ingredient quantity.
  4. Develop the controller hierarchy. Connected the mobile app to the ESP32, then used the ESP32, master Arduino, and slave Arduinos to distribute control tasks.
  5. Develop dispensing mechanisms. Used a stepper-and-screw mechanism for spices, pumps for liquids, and actuators for the ingredient containers.
  6. Add feedback and safety checks. Monitored spice and liquid levels with ultrasonic sensors, used a float switch in the liquid system, and included refill prompts when supplies were low.
  7. Integrate cooking control. Coordinated the hot plate, ingredient release, stirrer, and K-type thermocouple so the system could maintain the intended cooking sequence until the recipe time expired.
  8. Test and refine the hardware. Tested the actuator with Arduino, completed the final ingredient-container design and manufacturing, then assembled the system for demonstration.

Operating Sequence

  1. Pair the mobile device with the system.
  2. Place an empty pot on the measuring area and restart the system.
  3. Select Cook now, choose the recipe, and select the required quantity.
  4. Measure the ingredients and refill any container flagged as low.
  5. Select Start Dispensing and wait for the Dispense complete message.
  6. Select Cook to start the hot plate, ingredient release, and stirring sequence.
  7. Maintain the cooking temperature until the recipe time expires, then switch the system off automatically.

Control and Sensing

The system was designed around closed-loop awareness of the cooking process. Ingredient quantities were calculated from the selected recipe and measured using the load-cell system. Ultrasonic sensors monitored ingredient levels, while the liquid subsystem also used a float switch. The K-type thermocouple supported temperature monitoring during cooking.

The presentation documents the intended sequence and system architecture; it does not provide measured dispensing accuracy, calibration constants, temperature targets, or quantified performance results.

Key Takeaways

  • Large mechatronic systems become easier to develop when control responsibilities are separated across master and slave controllers.
  • Accurate measurement and reliable calibration are essential before proportional dispensing can be trusted.
  • Mechanical mechanisms, electronics, software, sensing, and user interaction must be designed as one integrated workflow.
  • Testing individual actuators and container mechanisms early reduces integration risk later.
