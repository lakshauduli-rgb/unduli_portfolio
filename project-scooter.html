Kick Scooter Manufacturing

A complete workshop build developed from a manufacturing plan through material selection, machining, woodworking, welding, forging, finishing, and final assembly.

Jan 2025 - May 2025 Manufacturing / Fabrication Group project Completed
Project file PRJ-04 Project type Group project My contribution Materials selection, machining, welding, woodworking, and final fabrication My role Manufacturing and fabrication team member Status Completed

Overview

This group project involved designing and fabricating a kick scooter from scratch in the university workshops. The build required us to translate a concept into a practical manufacturing plan, select suitable materials for each component, and combine several workshop processes into one reliable assembly.

My contribution covered material selection, workshop fabrication, machining, welding, and the final build. The project developed both technical manufacturing skills and the planning, coordination, and teamwork needed to complete a physical product.

Manufacturing Process

  1. Plan the build. Created a detailed manufacturing plan, breaking the scooter into components and selecting materials and processes according to function, cost, strength, and manufacturability.
  2. Fabricate the base and wheels. Used woodworking techniques to shape the base platform and wheels, preparing the main riding structure for assembly.
  3. Machine the front-handlebar hardware. Used lathe operations to manufacture the nuts and head hubs for the front handlebar, including precise thread cutting for secure fitting.
  4. Join the structural frame. Applied arc welding to connect the structural components securely and create a rigid scooter frame.
  5. Forge the front fork. Used forging techniques to produce a strong and reliable front fork capable of supporting the handlebar and front wheel assembly.
  6. Make and refine washers. Cut custom washers from metal sheets, then refined their edges and surfaces through grinding for a better fit and finish.
  7. Complete the final finish. Prepared the assembled scooter for painting and applied final finishing touches to create a clean, polished result.
  8. Inspect and assemble. Checked component fit, structural joints, moving parts, and overall alignment before completing the final scooter assembly.

Key Processes and Highlights

  • Material selection based on functionality, cost, strength, and manufacturability.
  • Woodworking for the base platform and wheels.
  • Lathe machining and precise thread cutting for nuts and front-handlebar head hubs.
  • Arc welding for secure structural joints.
  • Forging for a strong and reliable front fork.
  • Custom washer production from metal sheet followed by grinding and refinement.
  • Painting and final finishing for a clean, polished product.

My Role and Learning

I worked as a manufacturing and fabrication team member, contributing across material selection, machining, welding, woodworking, and final fabrication. Working through the complete build helped me understand how design decisions affect workshop processes, tolerances, assembly order, and the quality of the final product.

The project taught us that successful manufacturing depends not only on individual workshop skills, but also on careful planning, coordination, communication, and teamwork. Each process had to support the next stage so that the final assembly remained strong, functional, and presentable.

Project Outcome

The completed scooter brought together woodworking, machining, thread cutting, welding, forging, grinding, painting, and assembly in one practical manufacturing project. It demonstrated our ability to plan a build, work safely through multiple workshop processes, and turn raw materials into a finished mechanical product.
